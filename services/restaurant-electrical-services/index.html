What this work involves

Kitchen equipment drives the electrical design. Ovens, fryers, warmers, refrigeration, dishwashers and ventilation each have specific circuit requirements, and collectively they represent most of the connected load. Adding a piece of equipment to an existing kitchen is a capacity question before it is an installation question.

The dining room is a different problem entirely. Lighting there is part of the concept — dimming, colour temperature and fixture selection all contribute to how the space feels at different times of day. A restaurant that looks right at dinner and wrong at lunch usually has a controls problem rather than a fixture problem.

GFCI protection is non-negotiable in food service areas. Wet floors, cleaning schedules and constant equipment movement make this among the highest-priority protective measures in the building.

Why this is not a DIY job

Restaurant kitchens run heavy loads in a hot, wet environment on a schedule that does not tolerate downtime. That combination rewards doing the electrical work properly and punishes doing it cheaply.

Safety notes

  • Wet floors and electrical equipment make GFCI protection critical rather than advisable.
  • Ventilation and hood equipment circuits are part of the fire safety system.
  • Panel access must remain clear despite storage pressure in back-of-house areas.

Benefits

  • Dining lighting with proper dimming and zoning
  • GFCI protection appropriate to a wet environment
