After a hail or wind event, most attention goes to the roof — reasonably so. But the same storm dents gutters, cracks gable siding, tears soffit panels, and flattens fence sections, and those often get handled months later by a different contractor.

We inspect the exterior as a whole, document what we find, and can address the roof and the rest of it as one project.

When exterior storm damage repair is worth looking into

Dented gutters and downspouts

Soft metal shows hail clearly and is a strong indicator for the roof above.

Cracked or punctured siding

Especially on gable ends facing the storm.

Torn soffit panels

Wind gets under the overhang and peels them out.

Fence sections down or leaning

Straight-line winds hit fences hard.

Small items that are easy to overlook and easy to fix.

In detail

The damage that gets found months late

After a hail event almost all the attention goes to the roof, reasonably enough. What tends to get missed is everything the same storm hit on the way down: dented gutters and downspouts, cracked gable siding two storeys up, soffit panels peeled back by uplift, bent vent housings, torn window screens, and fence sections pushed flat.

Those are rarely urgent, which is exactly why they slide. Six months later the homeowner is arranging a second contractor, a second visit, and a second round of documentation for damage that could have been recorded in the same hour as the roof.

We inspect the exterior as one system and itemize the estimate by area, so you can take it in one go or stage it — but at least you are deciding with the whole picture in front of you.

Why gable siding is the usual casualty

Most North Texas homes are predominantly masonry with siding limited to gables, dormers, and rear elevations. Those sections sit high, face the weather, and are almost never examined from the ground.

They are also where wind-driven hail hits at the steepest angle. Cracked or punctured panels there let water behind the cladding, and because there is no ceiling directly below, nothing stains and nothing announces itself until the sheathing has been wet for a season or two.

Exterior Storm Damage Repair FAQs

Do you inspect more than the roof after a storm?

Yes. Gutters, fascia, soffit, siding, and fencing are all part of a proper post-storm exterior inspection.

Can exterior damage be handled with the roof work?

Usually, and it is generally simpler that way. The estimate is itemized so you can decide what to do and when.
