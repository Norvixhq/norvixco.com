What this work involves

Rough-in comes first and largely determines the outcome. Circuits, boxes, lighting positions and data runs go in before walls close, and anything missed becomes an expensive retrofit against a deadline. Time spent on the layout before rough-in is repaid several times over.

Coordination is the other half. Electrical work interleaves with framing, drywall, HVAC, ceiling grid and finishes, and the sequence matters. A build-out where the trades are working around each other rather than against each other finishes on time.

Why this is not a DIY job

On a build-out, the electrical scope is defined weeks before anyone notices it was defined wrong. Getting the rough-in right is the difference between a smooth finish and a scramble.

Safety notes

  • Temporary power on a construction site needs GFCI protection.
  • Work must be coordinated so circuits are not energised while other trades are in the ceiling.

Benefits

  • Layout planned before rough-in rather than corrected afterwards
  • Coordination with the other trades on site
  • Capacity confirmed before commitments are made
  • Documentation for landlord and inspection purposes
