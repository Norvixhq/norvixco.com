Emergency Board-Up & Roof Tarping

An open building is a building that keeps getting worse. Board-up and tarping stop weather, animals and intruders from turning one loss into a second one — and most policies require you to do it.

Why this cannot wait until morning

Three things happen to an open structure. Weather gets in — and in North Texas the next storm cell is rarely far away, turning a roof repair into a ceiling, insulation, drywall and flooring job. Animals get in, from raccoons and possums to birds and insects, and they do a surprising amount of damage quickly. And people get in, which is both a theft and a liability problem.

There is a fourth reason, and it is the one people find out about late. Nearly every property policy contains a duty to protect the property from further loss after a covered event. Additional damage that occurred because an opening was left unsecured can be excluded from the claim.

What we do

Window and door board-up

Openings covered with plywood cut to fit and secured to the structure, sized and fastened to hold against wind rather than tacked on.

Roof tarping

Heavy-duty reinforced tarp installed over compromised roofing, secured to the deck and lapped to shed water rather than pond it. A tarp that ponds is worse than no tarp.

Structural openings

Larger openings from fire, impact or collapse framed and closed to restore the building envelope.

Temporary fencing and access control

For significant losses and unoccupied properties where the structure itself cannot be fully secured.

Documentation

Photographs of the damage before board-up and of the completed work afterward, so the emergency mitigation is properly recorded on your claim — these costs are typically reimbursable.

What comes next

Board-up buys time; it is not a repair. It holds while the claim is opened, the scope is assessed and permanent work is scheduled. Because we handle mitigation and reconstruction, the same company that secured the property carries it through to the finished repair.

Your policy requires this

Nearly every property policy contains a duty to protect the property from further damage after a loss. It is not a suggestion. If a roof is left open through a second rain event, or a broken door lets someone walk in, a carrier can reasonably decline the portion of the damage that occurred after you had the opportunity to prevent it.

The corollary is the useful part: reasonable emergency protection costs are generally reimbursable under the same claim. Boarding and tarping is one of the few restoration expenses that reliably pays for itself.

Openings

Doors and windows are secured with plywood cut to the opening and fastened to structure rather than to trim, because trim tears out under leverage and a panel screwed to a casing is a closed door rather than a secure one. Where an opening is large — a storefront, a garage, a blown-out slider — we frame a backing structure first. Ground-floor and rear openings are treated as the priority, since that is where entry actually happens.

For commercial frontage where appearance matters and the property is still trading, we can board the interior line or use clear polycarbonate so the space still reads as open rather than abandoned.

Roof tarping

A tarp thrown over a hole and weighted with whatever is on the roof lasts until the next gust. A tarp installed properly is battened — the leading edge wrapped around a furring strip and fastened above the damage line so water cannot track under the fastener row, with the covering extending well past the damage on all sides and laid to shed with the slope rather than across it. Seams are lapped in the direction of flow.

Done correctly, a battened tarp holds for weeks in North Texas weather and survives the storm that follows the storm — which, in spring, is usually four days later.

Structural shoring

Where fire, impact or water load has compromised framing, temporary shoring goes in before anyone works underneath. This is judgment work and it is the part of an emergency response that is worth having a professional do, because a fire-weakened member can look sound and carry nothing.

Fencing, access control and weather sealing

After a significant fire or a partial collapse, temporary fencing keeps the site out of reach of the curious and reduces your liability exposure. Where a property is uninhabitable and will sit for weeks, we can also weather-seal the envelope properly — sealing openings against wind-driven rain, and where power is out, addressing the freeze risk to plumbing that turns one loss into two.

Temporary means temporary

Board-up and tarping buy time; they do not fix anything. A tarp is a covering, not a roof, and plywood is not a door. The clock on temporary protection runs in weeks, and the goal from day one is to get the permanent repair scoped, approved and started before the temporary measures are being asked to do more than they were built for. Because we carry the reconstruction as well, the board-up crew and the rebuild crew are the same company — there is no gap where the property sits secured and forgotten.

Documenting the property while it is open

The window between a loss and the board-up going on is the last chance to record what the property looked like. Once plywood is over the openings, nobody can see in, and the adjuster who arrives eleven days later is assessing a sealed building.

We photograph before, during and after the securing work — the damage itself, the interior condition, the point of entry or failure, and the completed protection. That set does two jobs: it evidences the extent of the original loss, and it evidences that you met your policy's duty to protect the property, which is what makes the emergency work reimbursable.

After a fire specifically

A fire-damaged property is secured differently. The fire service will have cut ventilation openings in the roof and forced entry doors, and those are usually the largest openings in the building. Utilities need to be confirmed off at the meter rather than at the panel. The structure needs assessing before anyone works on it, because fire-weakened framing does not announce itself.

There is also a security consideration people find unpleasant but should know: fire-damaged properties standing open are targeted, and the loss of undamaged belongings from a house that already burned is common enough to plan around. Securing the building the same day is the point at which the situation stops getting worse.

How quickly we can be there

Board-up and tarping are among the fastest things we do, because the work does not need equipment staged or a scope agreed — it needs materials and a crew. We carry both. If you call with an open building, that call gets treated as an active emergency regardless of the hour, and securing the property happens before anything else is discussed.

Cost

What drives the price of emergency board-up

This is one of the cheaper things you will do after a loss, and one of the most reliably reimbursable. We give you a written estimate free, before any work begins — but these are the factors that move the number, so you know what you are being quoted on.

Number and size of openings

Standard windows and doors versus storefront glass or a blown-out garage opening.

Roof tarping area

Square footage covered, roof pitch and how much battening is required to hold it.

Structural shoring

Whether temporary support is needed before anyone can safely work.

Materials

Plywood and fasteners, or polycarbonate where the property is still trading.

Fencing

Temporary site fencing where a property will stand open for an extended period.

We do not publish fixed prices, because a number quoted without seeing the property would be a guess. What we will do is walk the job with you, explain what we have found, and put the scope in writing at no cost.

Emergency Board-Up FAQs

Board-up and tarping are dispatched 24 hours a day, and in most cases we are on site the same day you call, including nights and weekends. Tell us what is open and we will give you a realistic arrival window for your address.

Emergency mitigation to prevent further loss is typically covered, and most policies require it. Keep documentation of the work; we provide before and after photographs and an itemized record for your claim.

You can for a ground-floor window if you have the materials and can do it safely. Roof tarping on a storm-damaged roof is a different matter — the deck may be compromised, it is usually wet, and a tarp that is not properly secured will blow off or pond water. That one is worth calling for.

Board-up is stable for extended periods. Tarps are a temporary measure and degrade in sun and wind — expect a few weeks to a couple of months depending on exposure. If permanent repair is delayed, tarps need inspection and replacement.
