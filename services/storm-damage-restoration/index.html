What North Texas weather actually does to buildings

DFW takes a specific set of hits: large hail in spring, straight-line winds that can match a weak tornado, occasional tornado activity, and intense short-duration rainfall that overwhelms drainage. Each damages a building differently.

Hail

Hail damage is often not visible from the ground. Impact fractures the shingle mat and strips the granules that protect the asphalt from UV. The roof may not leak for a year or more, but the shingle's service life has been cut short and failure is now a matter of time. This is why post-storm roof inspection matters even when nothing is obviously wrong — and why carriers impose deadlines for reporting hail claims.

Straight-line winds

More common here than tornadoes and capable of similar damage over a wider area. Winds lift shingle edges and break the adhesive seal, so the roof looks intact but is no longer sealed — the next ordinary rain drives water under the courses. Winds also take down fences, trees and limbs, and turn loose objects into projectiles.

Rainfall and drainage

Our clay soil sheds water rather than absorbing it, so a heavy cell produces fast runoff. Water backs up at overwhelmed gutters, finds compromised flashing, and pushes into low-lying garages, sunrooms and slab-on-grade additions.

Freeze events

Less frequent but very costly. Regional hard freezes burst uninsulated attic and exterior-wall piping across thousands of homes at once, and the damage appears everywhere simultaneously the moment temperatures rise.

First priority: stop the intrusion

After a storm, the damage that has already happened is fixed. The damage still to come is not, and it is usually larger. A roof opening that stays open through the next rain converts a roof repair into a ceiling, insulation, drywall, flooring and potentially mold job.

Emergency roof tarping

Heavy-duty tarp secured to the deck over the compromised area, installed to shed water rather than pond it. This is a temporary measure that holds until permanent repair, and it is the single highest-value thing that can happen in the first 24 hours.

Board-up

Broken windows, damaged doors and openings in the envelope closed to keep out weather, animals and intruders.

Debris removal

Limbs, fence sections and displaced material cleared so the structure can be assessed and worked on safely.

Water mitigation

Everything that came in through the opening is handled as a water loss: extraction, moisture mapping, structural drying and monitoring. Storm jobs that skip this step reliably become mold jobs.

Assessment and repair

Once the property is stable we document thoroughly — roof, exterior envelope, windows and openings, interior water damage, and contents — and produce a scope covering both what is visible and what testing revealed. Repairs run from roofing, decking, flashing, gutters, siding, soffit and fascia through to interior drywall, insulation, flooring, paint and trim.

Storm claims: what to do and what to watch out for

Report promptly. Texas policies commonly include reporting deadlines for hail and wind, and late reporting is one of the most common grounds for reduction or denial. Photograph everything before any temporary repair, then photograph the temporary repair too. Keep receipts for tarps, board-up and anything you buy to protect the property — those costs are typically reimbursable.

Every major DFW storm brings out-of-state crews who go door to door within days. Some are legitimate. Many are not. Be extremely cautious with anyone who shows up uninvited, pressures you to sign on the spot, offers to "waive" or "eat" your deductible, or asks you to sign a document assigning your claim benefits before you have read it. Absorbing a deductible is insurance fraud in Texas, and it makes you a party to it.

We are based in Dallas and we are here after the storm season ends, which is the relevant difference. Ask any contractor for a physical local address, a Texas contact and proof of insurance before signing anything — including us.

Hail damage is often invisible from the ground

The damage that matters on a composition shingle roof is not a hole. It is bruising — impact that fractures the mat beneath the surface and dislodges the granules protecting the asphalt from ultraviolet light. The shingle looks intact from the driveway and it is no longer weatherproof. Granule loss accelerates, the asphalt degrades, and the roof begins leaking one to three years later, long after the storm date and long after the claim window closed.

The reliable indicators are soft metal accessories — gutters, downspouts, vents, flashing, air-conditioner condenser fins, mailboxes and window screens. Hail leaves visible dents in soft metal at the same impact energy that bruises shingles. If the condenser fins are dimpled, the roof took the same hits.

This is the reason we recommend an inspection after any significant hail event in your area even if nothing appears wrong. Establishing damage on a date is straightforward; establishing it three years later against a carrier's argument that it is wear is not.

Wind, and the uplift sequence

Wind damage progresses in a predictable order. Uplift breaks the adhesive seal between shingle courses, and a shingle that is sealed only at the nail line lifts in every subsequent wind. Water then drives under the course during rain, reaches the decking, and the failure is well established before anything shows on a ceiling.

Missing shingles are the obvious sign. Lifted-but-not-missing shingles are the common one, and they are visible from a ladder and invisible from the ground.

The first 48 hours

Cover the roof properly — a battened tarp, fastened above the damage line, extending well past the affected area. Photograph everything before any temporary work goes on, including the debris and the ground-level indicators. Note the date and time of the storm, which your carrier will ask for. Move contents out from under active leaks and get containers under drips. Keep every receipt for emergency measures.

Then get the claim opened. Carriers work through post-storm volume in the order it arrives, and after a major DFW event the queue is measured in weeks.

Storm-chasing contractors

Within 72 hours of any significant hail event, out-of-state crews work affected neighbourhoods door to door. Some are competent. Many are not, and a proportion are running a business model that depends on being gone before the workmanship fails.

The specific warnings: anyone pressuring you to sign on the doorstep; anyone offering to waive, absorb or cover your deductible, which is illegal in Texas and makes you party to insurance fraud; anyone asking for an assignment of benefits before you have read it, which transfers control of your claim to them; anyone who cannot give you a physical local address; and anyone requesting substantial payment before materials are delivered.

Ask any contractor — us included — for a local address, current proof of general liability insurance, and references from work in your own city. A company that will still be here in five years to honour a workmanship warranty can answer all three in a minute.

Cost

What drives the price of storm damage restoration

Storm work is usually an insurance job, and the cost question is really a coverage question. We give you a written estimate free, before any work begins — but these are the factors that move the number, so you know what you are being quoted on.

Roof damage extent

Repairable sections versus full replacement, and whether decking underneath is affected.

Water intrusion

How far water travelled inside once the envelope was breached.

Emergency protection

Tarping and board-up, which is generally reimbursable under your policy.

Exterior components

Siding, gutters, windows, fencing and outbuildings are often damaged alongside the roof.

Code upgrades

Current code may require decking, underlayment or ventilation that the original roof did not have.

Interior finishes

Ceilings, insulation and flooring affected by the intrusion.

We do not publish fixed prices, because a number quoted without seeing the property would be a guess. What we will do is walk the job with you, explain what we have found, and put the scope in writing at no cost.
