The blind spot

Your monthly payment is not your cost.

Two drivers with the same $670 payment can have wildly different real costs. Depreciation, insurance, fuel and repairs often add more than the loan itself — and none of it shows up on the finance contract.

What the dealer advertises
$670/mo
The financed payment on a $34,000 SUV, with tax and fees rolled in. This is the number most people compare — and the only one they plan around.
What it actually costs to drive
$982/mo
The same SUV, all-in: depreciation, insurance, fuel, maintenance, taxes and interest. That's 47% more than the payment — about $18,700 in cost across five years that the finance contract never mentions.

Questions

The short version.

What is the "true cost" of owning a car?
It's every dollar the vehicle pulls out of your life, not just the loan. That includes depreciation (usually the largest cost), financing interest, insurance, fuel or electricity, maintenance and repairs, plus taxes, registration and fees. Our True Cost to Own calculator adds them all and divides by the miles you drive to give you a real cost per mile.
Why is depreciation counted as a cost?
Because it's real money you lose. If you buy for $34,000 and sell for $14,000 five years later, that $20,000 gap is the price you paid to use the car — often more than fuel, insurance and maintenance combined. It just doesn't show up as a monthly bill, which is exactly why people miss it.
Are these calculators really free?
Yes. No signup, no email wall, no lead forms. We don't sell cars, loans or insurance. The tools are free to use and the results are yours to share or print.
How accurate are the estimates?
Our defaults are grounded in EPA fuel-economy data, IRS and AAA driving-cost studies, and observed depreciation curves — but every vehicle and driver is different. That's why every assumption is editable. Treat the output as a well-informed estimate to guide your decision, not a quote. The methodology page sets out every formula, default and source.
Do you cover electric and hybrid vehicles?
Yes. The True Cost to Own calculator models gas, hybrid and fully electric powertrains — switching fuel to electricity (¢/kWh and efficiency) and adjusting maintenance and depreciation accordingly.

More answers are on the full FAQ, and the reasoning behind every default is set out in the methodology.
