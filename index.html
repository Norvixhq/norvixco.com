Storm restoration

After a North Texas storm,
get it documented.

Hail damage often does not leak for a year. By then the connection to the storm is far harder to establish, and any timeline in your policy has usually run out.

  • Hail damageImpact bruising and granule loss, frequently invisible from the ground.
  • Wind damageBroken seals, creased shingles, and uplift along rakes and ridges.
  • Missing or lifted shinglesExposed underlayment and shingles that no longer bond.
  • Roof leaksActive intrusion traced to its real entry point, not the nearest stain.
  • Fallen debrisLimb impact, punctures, and damaged decking.
  • Emergency tarpingTemporary protection to stop further water damage while a repair is scheduled.
  • Post-storm inspectionsDocumented assessment while the evidence is fresh.

Are your inspections and estimates really free?

Yes. Free inspections and free estimates, with no obligation. You keep the photo documentation whether or not you hire us.

How do I know if my roof has storm damage?

Usually you cannot tell from the ground, which is the whole problem. Dented gutters, granule wash in the driveway, and shingle debris in the yard are good hints. A free inspection settles it.

Do I need a full replacement or will a repair work?

It depends on how widespread the damage is and how much life the roof has left. If a repair will genuinely hold, we will tell you that — even though the replacement would be the bigger invoice.

How far do you travel from Dallas?

Dallas–Fort Worth is our core market, and we work throughout surrounding North Texas. We will travel roughly two hours in any direction from Dallas for the right project. Call with your address and we will give you a straight answer.

Will you handle my insurance claim?

No. We are not a public adjuster, attorney, or insurer. We can document visible damage, explain the roofing scope, and provide project information that you may share with your carrier. The claim itself is between you and your insurance company.

How long does a roof replacement take?

Many single-family homes are a one to two day job once materials are on site, depending on size, pitch, complexity, and weather. You will get a realistic window with your estimate.

What happens to the mess and the nails?

Debris goes into a container on site and leaves with us, and we run magnets across the yard, beds, and driveway. Nails in tires are the most common complaint about roofing crews and it is entirely preventable.

Who actually inspects my roof?

A licensed inspector. Where a roof is too steep or too fragile to walk safely, we document it with drone imagery instead — which gives a better record anyway.
