Property types we work in

  • Retail — storefronts, shopping centers and strip retail, where a closed door is lost revenue every hour.
  • Office — single suites through multi-floor, including server rooms and document recovery.
  • Restaurants and food service — kitchen fires, grease and hood incidents, walk-in failures, with health department requirements in play.
  • Multi-family and HOA — apartments, condos and townhomes, where a single unit's loss usually affects several others and coordination with residents matters as much as the work.
  • Warehouse and industrial — large-footprint drying, inventory protection and dock-area work.
  • Medical and professional — environments with additional sanitation and compliance requirements.
  • Hospitality — floor-by-floor and wing-by-wing work so rooms keep selling.

Scoped for continuity

Commercial work is planned around your operation, not the other way around. That means overnight and weekend scheduling, phasing so unaffected areas keep running, temporary containment and walkways that let customers and staff move safely around the work, and clear communication to tenants or residents so we are not creating a second problem for your front desk.

Documentation for two kinds of coverage

Commercial policies typically carry both property coverage and business interruption coverage, and BI claims are settled on evidence of how long the interruption lasted and why. We provide dated documentation of conditions, daily progress records, drying logs, equipment counts and completion timelines — the record that supports the BI portion, not just the repair invoice.

Property managers and portfolios

If you manage multiple properties, having one vendor with your building details, access procedures, insurance contacts and preferred finishes on file removes a great deal of friction at 2am. We are happy to set that up in advance rather than at the moment of a loss.

The real cost is rarely the building

On a commercial loss the physical damage is often the smaller number. The larger number is the revenue that stops, the staff who cannot work, the customers who go elsewhere and do not come back, and the lease obligations that continue regardless. Every decision we make on a commercial job is weighed against that clock.

Practically, that means we will often run more equipment than a strict calculation requires, work overnight, and phase the job so the highest-revenue areas return to service first — even when a different sequence would be simpler for us.

Phasing and partial occupancy

Most commercial properties do not need to close entirely, and the goal is to keep as much of the operation running as the loss allows. That requires hard containment separating work areas from occupied areas, negative air machines so construction dust and any contamination move away from staff and customers, protected access routes, and inspection-ready egress and life-safety paths at all times.

Retail, medical and food-service tenants each add their own constraints. Restaurants have health-department requirements that govern when the space can reopen. Medical and dental practices have infection-control obligations and often equipment that cannot tolerate the humidity swings that aggressive drying creates. We plan around those rather than discovering them mid-job.

Multi-tenant buildings and the vertical problem

Water in a multi-storey building is a vertical event. A supply failure on the fifth floor of a Las Colinas or Uptown tower affects five, four and three, and the tenants below rarely know anything is wrong until the ceiling grid starts dripping. The technical work is the same as anywhere; the coordination is not.

Multi-tenant losses involve building management, several tenants, multiple insurance carriers and often a property manager and an owner with different priorities. We document by unit, keep each affected party informed about their own space, and provide separate scoping where separate carriers are involved — because a single blended invoice across four policies is a claim that will not settle cleanly.

Large-loss capability

Commercial square footage changes the equipment class. Desiccant dehumidification rather than refrigerant, because desiccants pull vapour pressure lower and are far more effective in large open volumes and cool conditions. Trailer-mounted units and generator power where building electrical cannot carry the load — and building electrical usually cannot. Air movers counted in dozens rather than a handful. Rented temperature control to hold conditions that make drying work.

Warehouse and industrial space brings its own problems: high ceilings that stratify humidity, racked inventory that must be moved or protected, and concrete slabs that release moisture slowly for weeks.

Documentation that supports a business interruption claim

Business interruption coverage pays for lost income during the period of restoration, and that period has to be evidenced. Our documentation supports it: dated arrival and start times, daily progress with drying data, photographic records of conditions preventing occupancy, area-by-area completion dates, and a clear record of decisions and approvals.

Commercial claims are scrutinised more closely than residential ones and the sums are larger. Thin documentation is expensive on this side of the business.

Who we work with

Office and professional buildings, retail and restaurants, warehouse and light industrial, medical and dental practices, schools and childcare, hotels, churches, apartment communities and HOA common areas across the Metroplex. We are used to working with property managers, facilities teams and regional maintenance staff, and we can work to a purchase-order process where that is how your organisation buys.

Working with your insurance and your leases

Commercial losses usually involve more than one policy. The building owner carries property coverage on the structure; tenants carry coverage on improvements, betterments and contents; and the lease determines who is responsible for what. It is common for a single water loss to touch three policies and for none of the parties to be certain which one applies to a particular line item.

We scope by area and by ownership so each carrier receives documentation for what their policy actually covers, rather than one blended invoice that every adjuster reduces. Where a lease is genuinely ambiguous, we will say so and let the parties resolve it — but the physical documentation will be clean enough that the resolution is about the lease language rather than about what happened in the building.

Preventive work between losses

Most commercial clients call us the first time during an emergency and stay for the boring work afterward, which is generally the more valuable relationship. That includes pre-loss planning — documented shutoff locations, a current call list, and a decision made in advance about who has authority to authorise emergency work at 2am, which is the single thing that most reliably delays a commercial response.

It also includes moisture inspections of known problem areas, post-storm roof assessments before leaks appear internally, and scheduled attention to the mechanical spaces where losses actually originate: water heater rooms, sprinkler risers, roof drains and HVAC condensate systems. A condensate pan overflow above a suspended ceiling causes a remarkable amount of damage for a component that costs very little to check.

Cost

What drives the price of commercial restoration

Commercial pricing weighs the cost of the work against the cost of the business being closed. We give you a written estimate free, before any work begins — but these are the factors that move the number, so you know what you are being quoted on.

Square footage and ceiling height

Volume, not floor area, determines the equipment load.

Speed requirement

Running more equipment and working overnight costs more and usually costs less than staying closed.

Equipment class

Desiccant dehumidification, trailer units and generator power for large or cold spaces.

Phasing and containment

Keeping part of the operation trading while work proceeds around it.

Specialty environments

Restaurants, medical and data spaces carry compliance requirements that shape the method.

Multi-tenant coordination

Separate scoping per unit where several carriers and leases are involved.

We do not publish fixed prices, because a number quoted without seeing the property would be a guess. What we will do is walk the job with you, explain what we have found, and put the scope in writing at no cost.
